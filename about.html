How I Got Here
(The Real Story).

I didn't grow up wanting to be a woodworker. I grew up in a house where if something broke, you fixed it yourself—or you lived without it. My old man had a table saw in the garage that was older than I was, and by the time I was 12, I knew how to use it without losing a finger. That was the bar.

After high school, I did the construction thing for a while. Framing, trim work, cabinetry installs—whatever paid. I was good at it, but I hated working on other people's timelines with other people's cheap materials. So I started taking side jobs. Cutting boards at first. Then coffee tables. Then someone asked if I could do a butcher block island, and I said "sure" even though I'd never done one that big before.

"You learn more from one custom commission than you do from a hundred production runs. Every piece of wood has an attitude, and you either figure out how to work with it—or you waste a $300 slab."

That was 40 years ago. I've been doing this full-time ever since. No employees. No partnerships. Just me, the shop, and a backlog of projects that keeps me busy six weeks out at any given time. I could scale up, hire people, run ads, do the whole growth thing. But then I'd spend half my day managing instead of building, and honestly? I'd rather be in the shop.

People ask why I don't automate more of the process. Because automation is for making the same thing over and over. I don't make the same thing twice. Your countertop isn't anyone else's countertop. Your island isn't a catalog item. Every piece is dimensioned for your space, finished for your use case, and built to actually survive the next 50 years—not just look good in an Instagram story.

I'm not precious about this stuff. It's wood. It's functional. It's meant to get used. But I do care that it's done right the first time, because I don't do warranty repairs on garbage I didn't build. If it leaves my shop, it's built to last. If it's not, I don't ship it.

What You Should Know
Before You Call.

I Don't Do Rush Jobs

If you need something "by next week," I'm not your guy. Good work takes time. I've got a queue, and the people ahead of you didn't cut in line, so neither do you. Rush orders cost 25% more and they still take at least two weeks minimum. If that's a problem, there are plenty of shops that'll crank something out fast—just don't call me when it warps in six months.

No Design-By-Pinterest

I get it—you saw something cool online and you want it for your kitchen. That's fine. But I'm not reverse-engineering some photo with zero measurements and three different wood species that don't actually play nice together. If you want something specific, bring me dimensions, wood preferences, and a realistic budget. Otherwise, let me design it based on what'll actually work in your space.

I'm Honest About Costs

Hardwood isn't cheap. Labor isn't cheap. If you're shopping on price alone, you're gonna have a bad time—either with me or with whoever undercuts me and delivers garbage. I charge what the work is worth. No hidden fees, no surprise markups. But also no haggling. The quote is the quote.

I Stand Behind My Work

If something's wrong with a piece I built—joint failure, finish defect, dimensional error—I'll fix it or replace it. No questions asked. But if you ignore the care instructions, leave the board sitting in water for three days, or use it as a cutting board when I told you it's oil-finished decorative, that's on you. Wood isn't indestructible, and I'm not a miracle worker.

Brandon, Florida. Been here 30 years. The shop's a standalone building behind the house—climate-controlled, dust collection system, full setup. I ship nationwide, but if you're local and want to pick up in person, that works too. Just don't show up unannounced. I'm probably covered in sawdust and not in the mood for surprise visits.

What I Do When I'm Not Building

Honestly? Not much. I fish sometimes. I've got a boat I take out when the weather's decent and the project queue isn't insane. My wife says I need hobbies that don't involve power tools, but she married a guy who spends 60 hours a week in a workshop, so I don't know what she expected.

Why People Keep Coming Back

Because I don't bullshit them. If a project's a bad idea, I'll tell you. If your budget won't get you what you want, I'll tell you that too. And when I say something will be done in six weeks, it's done in six weeks—not eight, not "we're still sourcing materials," not "we'll get back to you." I do what I say I'm gonna do, and apparently that's rare enough now that people appreciate it.

The Workshop Playlist

Classic rock, mostly. Zeppelin, Stones, some Skynyrd when I'm in a mood. Occasionally my wife sneaks in and changes it to country, and I'll work for two hours before I realize. I'm not picky—as long as the table saw's louder than the music, I'm good.
